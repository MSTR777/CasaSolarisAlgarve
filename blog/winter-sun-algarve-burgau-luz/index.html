Winter Sun in the Western Algarve: Why Burgau and Luz Shine from November to February

Mild afternoons, nearly ten hours of daylight and a coastline made for walking, cycling and golf.

By the Casa Solaris hosts 8 November 2026 6 min read

At a glance

  • Daytime highs of about 16–19 °C from November to February, and warmer still in the sun.
  • Nearly ten hours of daylight even in December, the shortest month of the year.
  • Five to six hours of sunshine a day in midwinter, and more than 300 sunny days a year.
  • The sea sits at about 15–18 °C: better for beach walks than long swims.
  • Coastal walking, cycling and three golf courses within 30 minutes of Burgau.

While much of Europe pulls on thermal layers and switches the lights on at four in the afternoon, the Western Algarve does something quietly different. The summer crowds have gone home, the light turns soft and golden, and the whitewashed fishing village of Burgau and its neighbour Praia da Luz settle back into the rhythm of the people who live here all year round.

This is not sunbed weather, and that is rather the point. Between November and February the Algarve is a place for clifftop walks, long bike rides, a round of golf, unhurried lunches and, if you can work remotely, a terrace desk with an Atlantic sky instead of a grey window. Here is what to expect.

The numbers: how warm and sunny is it?

Western Algarve, typical monthly averages, rounded. Temperatures are measured in the shade.
Month Daytime high Sunshine a day Daylight
November about 19 °C about 6 h about 10 h 10 min
December about 17 °C about 5 h about 9 h 40 min
January about 16 °C about 5–6 h about 9 h 55 min
February about 17 °C about 6 h about 10 h 45 min

Those are shade temperatures, which is what forecasts and weather apps report. In direct sun and out of the wind, on a terrace or a sheltered beach, it feels several degrees warmer, and many winter afternoons are shirtsleeve weather. The days stay long too: even in December the sun is up for nearly ten hours, and by February it is closer to eleven.

We will be straight with you, though: it is still winter. The Algarve averages around five to six hours of sunshine a day in December and January, rising in February. December is the wettest month, with rain on eight to twelve days, usually in short showers rather than all-day downpours. The Atlantic sits at about 15–18 °C, which is refreshing to look at and bracing to enter. Pack layers and a light waterproof and you will be well prepared.

Walk the coast on the Fishermen’s Trail

Burgau lies directly on the Fishermen’s Trail (Trilho dos Pescadores), the coastal route of the Rota Vicentina, waymarked with blue and green stripes. The stretch between Burgau and Praia da Luz is among the gentlest: mostly flat, with a single hill and cliff-top views all the way. Short loops around Burgau and the Cliffs of Luz run to roughly 3.5–5 km and take one to one-and-a-half hours at a relaxed pace.

Want a longer day? Luz to Lagos is about 12 km (three to four hours), while the section from Burgau towards Salema is steeper, with more ups and downs. Walkers generally rate autumn as the best season for the trail; winter is quieter and still very pleasant, as long as you keep an eye on the weather, the wind and the cliff edges. There is no fee and no permit.

Cycle the Atlantic coast

The Algarve is mild enough that one of the early stage races of the professional season, the Volta ao Algarve, is held here every February (the 2026 edition ran from 18 to 22 February; dates for 2027 are still to be confirmed). For the rest of us, the main artery is the Ecovia do Litoral, part of EuroVelo 1 (the Atlantic Coast Route): about 214 km from Vila Real de Santo António to Cabo de São Vicente, passing through Lagos on the way to Sagres. West of Lagos the landscape grows wilder and more dramatic.

Closer to home, the municipality of Vila do Bispo opened a new 26-km ecovia between Sagres and Aljezur in January 2025, and bikes can be rented in Lagos.

Tee off in January

Three well-known courses sit within a short drive of Burgau, all roughly 15–30 minutes away by car:

  • Palmares: 27 holes with ocean views above the Bay of Lagos, widely regarded as one of the best courses in the Western Algarve.
  • Boavista: an 18-hole, par-71 course on the headland between Lagos and Luz, with views from Praia da Luz along the coast towards Sagres.
  • Espiche: an 18-hole, par-72 layout set within a nature reserve, with a strong sustainability focus.

Green-fee seasons change month by month, so check rates and book tee times ahead for the dates you want.

Two villages in their quiet season

Without the crowds, Burgau and Luz show their everyday character: fishermen’s boats on the sand, a coffee in the sun, a long lunch with no queue. To be honest about it, a few seasonal beach bars close for the winter and Burgau is the more seasonal of the two, so check opening hours before you set out. In Luz most shops and restaurants stay open, and Lagos, about 15 minutes away, has its marina, old town and year-round restaurants.

The winter calendar has its own highlights. From mid-January the almond trees begin to blossom across the Algarve countryside (the region’s Almond Blossom Festival is held in Alta Mora, at the eastern end, so it makes a day trip by car). February brings the Loulé Carnival and the Volta ao Algarve. Vila do Bispo, the municipality Burgau belongs to, has also hosted a late-winter festival of astronomy and music, and the dark skies here are made for stargazing. Dates change from year to year; the regional 365 Algarve programme (365algarve.pt) is the place to check.

Frequently asked questions

Can you swim in the sea in winter?

The Atlantic is about 15–18 °C, so it is cool rather than warm. Most winter visitors prefer to walk the beach and take the occasional bold dip.

How rainy is winter in the Algarve?

December is the wettest month, with rain on eight to twelve days, often as short showers. Sunny spells are frequent, and February is usually brighter.

Are restaurants and shops open in winter?

Most remain open in Luz and Lagos; a few seasonal places in Burgau and on the beaches close. Check opening hours in advance.

Do I need a car?

Burgau’s beach, restaurants and the coastal path are walkable. A car makes Lagos, the golf courses and Sagres much easier.
